EXPERIMENT 01
Newsletter Subscription and Brand Analysis
Lab manual reference: pages 1-5
AIM
To subscribe to a weekly or quarterly newsletter and analyze how its content and structure help in
building brand awareness and attracting potential customers.
PROBLEM DESCRIPTION
A newsletter is a marketing tool used by companies to share information, updates, offers and promotions.
It helps maintain communication with existing customers and attract new customers. In this study, we
analyze a travel newsletter and examine how its content and design support company branding.
DIGITAL MARKETING STRATEGY
Case study: Where Baltimore travel magazine. This seasonal travel magazine provides recommendations
about tourist attractions, shopping, dining, entertainment and local transportation.
1. Content analysis
• Recommendations: Popular places for first-time visitors, couples and luxury travelers.
• Shopping: Department stores, shopping areas and local boutiques.
• Museums and attractions: Historical buildings, museums and cultural centres.
• Dining: Restaurants and local food recommendations.
• Entertainment: Theatres, music, events and seasonal activities.
• Navigation: Maps, local guidance and transportation information.
2. Structural analysis
• Attractive headings, images and a clear table of contents.
• Sections arranged by category for easy reading.
• Addresses, contact details and maps for planning visits.
• Social media links to encourage further engagement.
3. Brand awareness and customer segments
• Useful, regular content increases recognition and trust.
• Pictures and a consistent layout make the brand memorable.
• Readers include tourists, families, couples, shoppers and food lovers.
PROCEDURE
1. Open a newsletter website or a digital magazine page.
2. Select a weekly or quarterly newsletter and subscribe if available.
3. Read the newsletter and identify its main content categories.
4. Observe its headings, images, links, contact details and page structure.
5. Identify likely customer segments for each category.
6. Describe how the newsletter improves engagement and brand recognition.
7. Record the analysis and prepare the lab report.

Observation: Organized sections, appealing pictures and useful recommendations make the newsletter relevant
to different audiences.
RESULT
Thus, the content and structure of a travel newsletter were analyzed to understand how newsletters
build brand awareness and attract potential customers.

VIVA QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
1. What is a newsletter? A newsletter is an email or publication that shares information and updates with
subscribers.
2. What is brand awareness? It means how easily people recognize and remember a company.
3. What is customer segmentation? It means dividing customers into groups based on their needs or interests.
4. What is email marketing? It is promoting products or services and maintaining customer relationships through
emails.

EXPERIMENT 02
Social Media Political Advertising: Impacts and Ethics
Lab manual reference: manual analysis format: pages 11-13 (Dove shampoo example adapted to political advertising)
AIM
To discuss the positive and negative impacts and ethical implications of using popular social media
applications for political advertising.
PROBLEM DESCRIPTION
Platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, YouTube and X can share political messages, election information
and public issue campaigns. Political advertising may increase public awareness, but misleading
information, manipulation and privacy risks can also arise.
DIGITAL MARKETING STRATEGY
1. Positive impacts
• Wide audience reach: Political information can reach many people quickly.
• Public awareness: Citizens can learn about elections, policies and public issues.
• Cost-effective communication: Online messages may cost less than traditional media.
• Public engagement: People can comment, share and discuss issues.
• Fast communication: Announcements can be communicated immediately.
2. Negative impacts
• Fake news: False claims can spread quickly.
• Misinformation: Edited videos or misleading posts can confuse people.
• Data privacy issues: Personal data may be misused or handled without permission.
• Social conflict: Harmful posts can increase division and hostility.
• Manipulation: Misleading advertisements can distort public understanding.
3. Ethical implications
• Transparency: Clearly identify the advertiser and sponsor.
• Honesty: Avoid false information and misleading claims.
• Privacy: Respect consent and protect personal information.
• Fairness: Do not promote hatred or discrimination.
• Accountability: Follow applicable election rules and platform policies.
PROCEDURE
1. Select popular social media platforms used for public information.
2. Observe publicly available political advertisements or awareness campaigns.
3. Identify the intended message and target audience.
4. List benefits such as reach and public participation.
5. Identify drawbacks such as fake news, manipulation and privacy risks.
6. Evaluate transparency, accuracy, fairness and accountability.
7. Compare the findings and prepare the report.

Observation: Social media supports broad political communication but requires truthfulness, transparency and
responsible data practices.
RESULT
Thus, the positive impacts, negative impacts and ethical implications of political advertising on social
media were studied and analyzed.

VIVA QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
1. What is political advertising? It is the promotion of political messages, policies, candidates or campaigns using
media.
2. Name two social media platforms. Instagram and Facebook.
3. What is misinformation? It is false or incorrect information shared with others.
4. What is ethical advertising? It means communicating honestly, fairly and responsibly.

EXPERIMENT 03
Case Study: Insurance Lead Generation and Conversion
AIM
To study how an insurance company generates, manages and converts potential leads into customers
using digital marketing strategies.
PROBLEM DESCRIPTION
Insurance companies need interested customers for their services. A lead is a person who shows interest
in a product or service. The challenge is to identify suitable leads, manage enquiries, build trust and
convert interested people into policyholders.
DIGITAL MARKETING STRATEGY
Case study: Life insurance company. The manual discusses company-provided leads, third-party lead
providers, LinkedIn and professional networking.
1. Lead generation
• Company leads: Potential customer contacts supplied by the company.
• Third-party leads: Contacts obtained through authorized lead providers.
• LinkedIn marketing: Share expertise and connect with professionals.
• Social media content: Publish useful information about policy benefits.
• Referrals: Ask satisfied customers for voluntary recommendations.
2. Lead management
• Collect details with permission and record customer requirements.
• Classify leads by interest and needs; store information in CRM software.
• Follow up respectfully and explain appropriate policy options.
3. Lead conversion and customer relationships
• Understand customer needs and answer questions.
• Provide suitable plans and help with the policy application process.
• Support customers after purchase and maintain meaningful contact.
PROCEDURE
1. Select a life insurance company as a case study.
2. Identify its online and offline lead generation sources.
3. Study the role of LinkedIn, referrals and professional networking.
4. Observe the methods used to record and prioritize customer enquiries.
5. Explain follow-up, policy discussions and conversion steps.
6. Identify how CRM improves customer relationships.
7. Prepare the case study analysis.

EXPERIMENT 04
Keyword Research for a Skincare or Dermatology Clinic
AIM
To perform keyword research for a skincare hospital or dermatology clinic website using Google
Keyword Planner and analyze keywords based on search volume, competition and relevance.
PROBLEM DESCRIPTION
A dermatology clinic wants patients to find its website when searching for skin care services. Good
keyword research helps the clinic understand patient searches, improve content and plan online
advertisements.
DIGITAL MARKETING STRATEGY
Tool: Google Keyword Planner in Google Ads.
• Keyword research: Find phrases such as "dermatology clinic", "acne treatment" and "skin specialist near
me".
• Search volume: Check average monthly searches for each keyword.
• Competition: Compare low, medium and high advertiser competition.
• Relevance: Prefer keywords that match the clinic's actual services.
• Long-tail keywords: Consider specific phrases like "best dermatologist for acne treatment in Nagercoil".
• Implementation: Use suitable keywords in service descriptions, website headings and ad campaigns.
Important: Google Keyword Planner competition is for advertisements. It is not a direct measurement of organic SEO
keyword difficulty.
PROCEDURE
1. Visit ads.google.com and sign in to Google Ads.
2. Open Tools / Planning / Keyword Planner (menu position may differ).
3. Choose "Discover new keywords".
4. Enter "skin treatment", "dermatology clinic" and similar terms.
5. Set the desired location and language.
6. Click "Get results" and inspect keyword ideas.
7. Review average monthly searches and advertiser competition.
8. Apply filters, select relevant keywords and record the results.
9. Use the selected keywords in website content and marketing campaigns.

RESULT
Thus, the process of keyword research for a skincare website using Google Keyword Planner was studied
to identify relevant keywords based on search volume and competition.

VIVA QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
1. What is a keyword? A word or phrase entered by a user into a search engine.
2. What is Google Keyword Planner? A Google Ads tool for finding keywords and viewing estimated search
demand and ad competition.
3. What is search volume? How often people search for a keyword during a period.
4. What is SEO? Search Engine Optimization, which helps a website appear in relevant unpaid search results.

EXPERIMENT 05
Google Search Indexing and URL Submission
AIM
To demonstrate how Google Search Console can be used to submit website URLs for indexing and
explain how Google discovers web pages.
PROBLEM DESCRIPTION
New websites and blog posts may not appear immediately in Google Search. Google must first discover
and crawl a page before deciding whether to add it to its search index. Google Search Console lets site
owners inspect URLs and request indexing when eligible.
DIGITAL MARKETING STRATEGY
Tools: Blogger, Google Search Console; the manual also includes a Google Cloud / Indexing API
workflow.
• Website creation: Publish a helpful blog post.
• Discovery: Google finds pages through links, sitemaps and other methods.
• Crawling: Googlebot requests and reads the page.
• Indexing: Google decides whether to store the page in its search index.
• Inspection: Use Search Console to check if a URL is indexed.
• Visibility: An indexed page may appear for relevant searches; indexing does not guarantee ranking.
PROCEDURE
1. Open blogger.com and sign in with a Google account.
2. Create a blog and choose its title and URL.
3. Create a new post with a title and useful content.
4. Publish the post and copy its full URL.
5. Open search.google.com/search-console.
6. Add the website property and complete ownership verification.
7. Paste the published post URL into "URL Inspection".
8. View the current status of the URL.
9. If the page is eligible and not indexed, select "Request Indexing".
10. Check the status again later and record the result.
